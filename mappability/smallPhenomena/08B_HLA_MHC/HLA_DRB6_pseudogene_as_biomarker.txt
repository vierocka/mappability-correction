HLA-DRB6 as a pseudogene in the APM biomarker gene list
=========================================================

Context
-------
Gong & Karchin (2023) — "Clustering by antigen-presenting genes reveals immune
landscapes and predicts response to checkpoint immunotherapy" (Sci. Reports 13:950)
— propose a 23-gene APM expression signature that includes HLA-DRB6.  The gene
was selected from an initial forward-selection process on bulk and scRNA-seq
transcriptomic cohorts.

HLA-DRB6 classification in GENCODE v49
---------------------------------------
Biotype : transcribed_unprocessed_pseudogene
Transcript (longest annotated) : ENST00000437650.2  (715 bp)
MANE Select : NONE  — no MANE transcript exists for HLA-DRB6
Confirmed in : HLApseudo_MAFFT/transcript_table.tsv  (is_pseudogene = True)

HLA-DRB6 carries inactivating mutations (stop codons / frameshift indels) and
is not predicted to produce a functional protein.  It retains promoter activity
and is detectably transcribed in normal tissue, particularly in immune cells,
explaining why it passes expression-based gene-selection filters.

Role in the HLApseudo_MAFFT analysis
--------------------------------------
In the MAFFT alignment project (HLApseudo_MAFFT/), the five DRB pseudogenes
(DRB2, DRB6, DRB7, DRB8, DRB9) were aligned against the four functional DRB
genes (DRB1, DRB3, DRB4, DRB5) as the classII_DRB group.

Key result from shared_fraction.tsv (≥90% identity threshold):

  Gene     W=75bp  W=100bp  W=150bp  W=250bp
  HLA-DRB1   61%     66%      72%      81%
  HLA-DRB4   62%     69%      71%      79%
  HLA-DRB3   56%     58%      61%      70%
  HLA-DRB5   49%     50%      55%      57%

At 100 bp read length, 66% of HLA-DRB1 sliding windows share ≥90% nucleotide
identity with at least one of the five DRB pseudogenes.  HLA-DRB6 is one of the
five that together produce this overlap — it cannot be isolated from the group
in the sliding-window analysis, but it is among the two longest pseudogenes in
the group (715 bp vs DRB2 at 503 bp) and is therefore the most likely source
of the competition for DRB1 reads.

The overlap worsens with longer reads (81% at 250 bp) because DRB pseudogenes
share exon 2 — which encodes the peptide-binding groove and is the most
conserved block — and longer reads more often span this block in full.

Implication for the biomarker paper
-------------------------------------
The paper measured "HLA-DRB6 expression" from bulk RNA-seq.  Three confounds:

1. Read contamination from functional DRB genes.
   Because DRB6 shares 60–80% of its exonic k-mers with DRB1/3/4/5, any
   standard aligner will assign some DRB1/3/4/5 reads to DRB6 (kallisto/Salmon
   via EM; STAR if multi-mapping is allowed).  The "expression" signal for DRB6
   is therefore a mix of genuine pseudogene transcription and leaked quantification
   from the functional genes.

2. Differential pseudogene derepression in cancer vs normal.
   The DRB locus in cancer (melanoma, as studied) may undergo chromatin
   remodelling that derepress pseudogene promoters.  If DRB6 is more expressed
   in some tumour subtypes, the additional pseudogene transcripts increase the
   ambiguous read pool and distort quantification of DRB1/3/4/5 in a
   sample-specific manner.  This is not a constant bias — it manufactures
   apparent fold-changes that do not reflect protein surface expression.

3. No MANE canonical transcript → no UF data in the mappability project.
   Because HLA-DRB6 has no MANE Select transcript, it is absent from the
   sc3prime_v5g_MANE_RNA_PE_100.tsv uniqueness-factor tables.  Our UF analysis
   correctly flags it as "not in MANE" — the practical consequence is that
   any study treating HLA-DRB6 as a coding gene will receive no mappability
   warning from the standard reference set and the ambiguity will go undetected.

Conclusion
----------
HLA-DRB6 inclusion in the APM signature is a methodological concern.  Its
measured expression likely represents a mixture of pseudogene transcription and
mappability leakage from functional DRB genes.  Prognostic signals attributed
to HLA-DRB6 in the Gong & Karchin model should be interpreted with caution;
they may reflect DRB1/DRB3/DRB4/DRB5 biology (via leakage) or tumour-specific
pseudogene derepression, rather than HLA-DRB6 protein function (which does not
exist).
