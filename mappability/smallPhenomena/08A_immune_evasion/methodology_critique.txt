Methodological critique — Shi et al. (2022)
============================================
"Role of GBP1 in innate immunity and potential as a tuberculosis biomarker."
Sci. Reports 12:11097.  doi:10.1038/s41598-022-15482-2

Core question
-------------
Does the GBP1-module signal arise from GBP1 specifically, or from broader
transcriptional activity of the IFN-γ / innate immune response — with the
gene-level decomposition being an artefact of probe cross-hybridisation?

Short answer: the biological signal (IFN-γ-driven innate immune activation in
TB) is almost certainly real.  The gene-specific interpretation — that GBP1,
HLA-B, ELF4, HLA-E, IFITM2, TNFRSF14, CD274, AIM2, CFB, RHOG, HORMAD1 are
individually meaningful biomarkers — is not supported by the methodology.


Platform: microarray, not RNA-seq
-----------------------------------
The data are from Illumina Sentrix HT12 V3 BeadChip arrays (50-mer oligonucleotide
probes), retrieved as pre-processed GEO datasets (GSE83456 training;
GSE42834, GSE19491 validation) using the R/Bioconductor package GEOquery.

Processing pipeline:
  RNA isolation  →  GLOBINclear globin reduction  →  Illumina CustomPrep amplification
  →  HT12 V3 hybridisation  →  BeadStudio v2 per-chip normalisation  →  log₂ transform
  →  limma (lmFit + eBayes) for DEG calling
  →  WGCNA on DEG intersection  →  LASSO on hub gene intensities

There is NO reference genome alignment.
There is NO read counter.
There is NO aligner.

The "expression values" throughout the paper (Fig. 1A box plots, heatmap, LASSO
index) are log₂ hybridisation intensity values from microarray probes, not
read-count-derived TPM or FPKM.


Critical omission: probe IDs are not reported
----------------------------------------------
The paper does not state which Illumina HT12 V3 probe ID was used for any gene.

This is a fundamental reporting failure.  For a gene family with multiple
paralogs sharing high sequence identity, the probe ID determines:
  - Whether the probe sequence falls in a conserved or divergent region
  - Which paralogs can cross-hybridise at standard hybridisation stringency
  - Whether the signal is gene-specific or family-level

Without probe IDs, the gene-level interpretation cannot be verified or
reproduced.  Reviewers cannot check specificity; readers cannot replicate.


Probe cross-hybridisation: the microarray equivalent of multi-mapping
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Illumina HT12 V3 uses 50-mer probes.  A 50-mer probe placed in a region
shared at >80% identity with a paralog will produce a mixed signal — the
microarray equivalent of a multi-mapping read.  This is not corrected for
in the paper.

  Gene       Family / issue
  ---------  -----------------------------------------------------------------
  GBP1       GBP family (GBP1–7) share 50–70% CDS identity.  GBP1, GBP2, GBP3,
             GBP4, GBP5 are adjacent on chr1p22.  A probe in a conserved exon
             block will hybridise to multiple family members simultaneously.
             GBP1 and GBP5 are both listed as interferon-stimulated genes
             (ISGs) in the paper itself — they are co-regulated and
             sequence-similar, making probe specificity critical and unverified.

  HLA-B,     HLA class I paralogs (HLA-A, B, C, E, F, G) share 75–90% identity
  HLA-E      in exons 2–3 (the peptide-binding groove).  Cross-hybridisation
             between class I probes is documented in the microarray literature.
             The LASSO model index literally includes HLA-B and HLA-E as
             separate terms, but their probe signals may both reflect a mixture
             of class I family expression.

  IFITM2     IFITM1, IFITM2, IFITM3 are local tandem duplicates on chr11p15,
             ~80% identical.  Probe specificity between family members is not
             guaranteed and not verified.

  TNFRSF14   TNF receptor superfamily (>25 members); some share conserved
             cysteine-rich domains at 60–70% identity.

  RHOG       Rho GTPase superfamily.  RHOG/RHOA/RHOB/RHOC share conserved
             GTPase domain sequences.  Probe placement matters.


The signal is real; the interpretation is wrong
-------------------------------------------------
This is the same structural problem as in 08B (Gong & Karchin 2023):

  The results are likely biologically real.
  TB patients have strongly activated IFN-γ signalling and innate immune
  responses.  GBP proteins, HLA class I, IFITM proteins, and AIM2
  inflammasome components are all bona fide IFN-γ target genes.  The
  upregulation measured in GSE83456 is real and reproducible.

  But the gene-specific interpretation is wrong.
  The WGCNA module captures the IFN-γ transcriptional programme — a
  co-regulated gene network — not individual gene contributions.  LASSO
  selection from a co-regulated, cross-hybridising gene set picks the
  strongest proxies for the composite IFN-γ signal, not the causal genes.

  The LASSO model index:
    GBP1×0.1568 + HLA-B×0.0845 + ELF4×0.0965 + TNFRSF14×0.0279
  should be read as: "weighted average of IFN-γ response intensity,
  measured with imprecise family-level probes", not as four specific
  protein contributions to TB pathology.

  The AUC = 0.97 on the training set and AUC = 1.0 on GSE42834 are
  consistent with the model capturing a strong, real biological signal
  (IFN-γ activation in TB) — not with it having identified the specific
  molecular mechanism.


What would be needed for gene-specific claims
----------------------------------------------
  1. Report probe IDs for every gene in the analysis.
  2. Verify probe specificity: BLAST each 50-mer against the transcriptome;
     flag probes with >80% identity hits to any paralog.
  3. For GBP1: use a probe in the divergent C-terminal helical domain
     (positions ~1400–1800 nt from TSS) where family members differ.
  4. For HLA-B/HLA-E: acknowledge that class I probes measure a mixture
     of class I family signal; do not use individual class I genes as
     separate LASSO predictors.
  5. Validate at the protein level (e.g., Western blot or proteomics for
     GBP1) before claiming GBP1 is a TB biomarker rather than a proxy
     for IFN-γ activation.


Comparison with 08B
--------------------
Both papers share the same logical structure:

  Real signal        →  IFN-γ / immune activation (08A), HLA locus activity (08B)
  Artefact           →  probe cross-hybridisation (08A), read multi-mapping (08B)
  Wrong conclusion   →  gene X is THE biomarker (08A), gene X drives prognosis (08B)

In 08A the technology is microarray (probe cross-hybridisation = the problem).
In 08B the technology is RNA-seq (read multi-mapping and EM redistribution = the problem).
The epistemological failure is identical: mistaking a co-regulated, non-uniquely
measurable locus signal for individual gene-level evidence.
